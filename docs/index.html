How the classical path works

The halo is a broad, low-frequency ring, while the cell body carries the high-frequency detail. Blurring at two scales separates them: σ₁ keeps the cell, σ₂ is set wide enough to straddle the halo, and subtracting a fraction α of their difference removes the ring without hollowing the cell out. CLAHE then restores local contrast in a way that plain histogram equalisation cannot, because its per-tile clip limit stops background noise from being amplified into the foreground.

Both are a few hundred lines of arithmetic, which is why they can run here on every drag. cv.js is a direct port of src/preprocessor.py; scripts/verify_web_pipeline.py runs both implementations over the same images and fails if they disagree by more than a set margin. At the time of writing they agree to ~50 dB PSNR (max per-pixel difference ≈ 0.016 of full scale), the remainder coming from tile-boundary interpolation.

Why the metrics look the way they do

Something worth noticing: the classical output usually scores worse PSNR than the untouched input, while the U-Net scores far better. That is not a bug. PSNR compares absolute intensities, and CLAHE deliberately rewrites the global tone curve — so an image that is visibly cleaner can move further from the target in absolute terms. The residual U-Net is trained against that target, so it stays on the intensity scale PSNR rewards. It is a good demonstration of why a single number is a poor proxy for "does this help my segmentation".

What runs here, and what does not

  • Live, in your browser: the DoG + CLAHE pipeline, and the PSNR/SSIM numbers, recomputed on every parameter change.
  • Precomputed: the residual U-Net column. At 31M parameters it is a ~124MB float32 download and far too slow for WebAssembly, so its output is rendered ahead of time by scripts/make_demo_assets.py and served as an image. Run it yourself with python main.py --mode hybrid.
  • Synthetic data: the samples come from data/generate_synthetic.py, which simulates halo artifacts over clean targets. Real phase-contrast microscopy has no paired ground truth, which is exactly why the project is built this way.
