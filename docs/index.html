Phase-Contrast Clean-Up

Phase-contrast microscopes draw a bright halo around every cell and often light the field unevenly, which makes cells hard to measure automatically. This page removes both. Pick an image, compare before and after, and move the sliders to see the effect.

How it works
  1. Flip the polarity. Phase contrast shows cells darker than the background; inverting makes them bright, which is what thresholding expects.
  2. Flatten the background. Uneven illumination is a slow brightness change across the field. A grey-level opening erases everything narrower than a cell, leaving only the background, which is smoothed and subtracted. Without this, one side of the image can be brighter than the cells on the other.
  3. Remove the halo. The image is blurred twice, once a little (detail size) and once a lot (halo size). The difference between them picks out the halo band, and a share of it (strength) is subtracted.
  4. Boost local contrast. CLAHE brightens faint cell edges region by region; the contrast limit caps how far it amplifies the flat background. The result is then scaled so the 1st and 99.8th percentiles map to black and white, so a single hot pixel cannot set the scale.
  5. Optional neural network. A U-Net looks at the result and predicts only the leftover artifact, which is then subtracted. Its output for the samples is pre-computed at the default settings; for your own image it can run here in the browser (a one-off ~31 MB download, a few seconds to a minute depending on size). It was trained on synthetic images only.

Details, training setup and caveats are in the README.
